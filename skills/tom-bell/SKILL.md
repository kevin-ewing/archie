# Tom Bell

Read the [Tom Bell dossier](../../docs/story/TOM_BELL.md) for his life, relationships and voice. Read the [story workshop](../../docs/story/README.md) for current campaign decisions and the [sixpence continuity](../../docs/STORY.md#the-sixpence) when writing across chapters. This skill lives in Archie and uses those project files as its references.

- Establish the date, audience and medium of the requested piece. The dossier is a snapshot immediately before Tom is introduced at Rook Field. Use only what he already knows and possesses at the scene’s date. His first artillery sortie, the arrival of the sixpence and its eventual return belong to campaign continuity. For another character’s dossier, use the same rule and take the snapshot immediately before their own introduction.
- Tom flies. Vale observes in Flanders in 1915 and Pike observes on the Somme in 1916. Keep airborne observation, ground-side decisions and who keys the wireless distinct. Write exact procedure only when established.
- Apply the dossier’s voice by deciding what Tom wants his reader or listener to do or understand. Use the proper register for the audience and the relevant details of his actual situation. In revision, check for balanced clauses that sound rehearsed, blanket aphorisms, polished emotional endings, strings of three, and exposition a recipient already knows. Allow variation in pace without adding decorative slang or phonetic spelling. The [editing reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) is a checklist for revision.
- For keepsakes, establish who an object connects Tom to and how it reached him. He notices condition, handwriting and what something came wrapped in. Show attachment in what he keeps together, carries, repairs, sends or returns. His interest can outlast an object’s usefulness.
- When updating the dossier, record lived events up to the moment before his introduction and the habits or characteristics they produced. Keep unapproved biography and future plot work in the workshop. Keep the voice guide descriptive and retain exactly one unrelated-to-war writing sample at the end. Use the sample to calibrate voice and compose fresh text for the requested situation.
- Check new writing against what Tom has actually seen or been told, which objects have reached him, and who can hear or read the words. Re-read once for character and once for ordinary speech before delivering.
