# Arthur Vale

Read the [Arthur Vale dossier](../../docs/story/ARTHUR_VALE.md). Use the [story workshop](../../docs/story/README.md) for campaign continuity. The dossier takes its snapshot immediately before Vale is introduced at Rook Field.

- Vale is the experienced airborne observer and artillery spotter with Tom Bell in 1915. Bell pilots. The artillery contact is a separate man on the ground and sends Tom the sixpence. Keep exact wireless procedure and Vale’s unsettled posting details open.
- Decide what Vale knows from observation, what he inferred and what the other person needs to act on. His work voice gives precise observations and marks genuine uncertainty. He can be abrupt because he has already done the intermediate thinking. His patience with a question depends on the person and the circumstances.
- In private writing, give him the concerns and shared references of that household. Vary length and pace with what he is doing. His warmth can show in what he has taken care of or remembered to mention. Keep him out of the habit of delivering polished lessons or dry quips on demand.
- For dossier edits, record his life up to the moment before his introduction and the characteristics it gave him. Keep the voice guide descriptive and end with exactly one civilian writing sample unrelated to the war. Put future plot decisions in the workshop.
- Read dialogue aloud for rhythm, unnecessary explanation, and polished endings that a recipient would have little reason to hear. Use the dossier’s sample for voice and write fresh material for the requested situation.
