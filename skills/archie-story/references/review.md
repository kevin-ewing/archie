# Review and continuity

Use for critique, revision, continuity checks, or a final pass on substantial story work. Scale the review to the request. Do not run a campaign audit for a punctuation edit.

## Diagnose before polishing

Work from the largest relevant cause toward the sentence:

1. **Purpose and causality:** What experience is promised? What causes the scene and what can it cause afterward? Does the proposed action belong to the current crew?
2. **Character and agency:** What does each person want? Who decides? Is a character serving only as a lesson, obstacle, or reward? Does the player have a clear role?
3. **Information and continuity:** What is known, inferred, received, possessed, or still unverified at this date? Could the player have understood the decision?
4. **Pacing and delivery:** Is there room to notice and react? Does the medium carry the intended information? Is essential meaning lost if optional content is skipped?
5. **Language:** Apply the existing character skills; check rhythm, audience, explanatory dialogue, and unearned emotional endings.

Describe an observable problem, its effect on the reader or player, and a concrete repair. Separate a continuity error from a preference. Quote only the short passage needed to locate an issue. Preserve the author's intended effect where possible, and identify a strength that a revision should retain when useful. Do not rewrite an entire scene because one turn is unclear.

For a “make it more profound” request, ask internally whether the player understands the competing needs, has reason to care, participates in the consequence, and has space to interpret it. An extra thematic speech rarely repairs missing setup.

## Continuity pass

For work spanning scenes or branches, keep a compact ledger only as detailed as needed:

| Event/date | Status | Who knows it and how | Object owner/location | Relationship or duty change | Later dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Named event | Established / proposed / unresolved / historical check | Observation, report, inference; received/read distinction | Transfer route and time if relevant | What actually changed | The beat relying on it |

Check especially:

- Vale and Pike are observers in different chapters, not the two ground operators of the old draft.
- Rooke's posting across the transfer and Vale's departure have not been silently decided.
- The player chooses message content without the draft pretending to settle who keys it.
- No grounded character replies by two-way radio or knows an unreported airborne event.
- Tom does not possess the sixpence before its delivery; returning it is postwar.
- Pike does not start with relationships or knowledge he has not acquired.
- A letter does not respond to an unread or unsent message; an official transcript does not quietly acquire corrected wording.
- A proposed ending does not treat outdated single-seat aircraft replacement as the established separation.
- Dossier changes remain before introduction and retain exactly one civilian sample at the end.

Do not resolve a conflict by choosing whichever file has the strongest heading. Use the workshop's explicit precedence. For a conflict it does not resolve, state the conflict and offer a provisional way forward rather than inventing certainty.

## Walk the story paths

For a branching scene or mission, follow its preconditions, decision, consequence, and next encounter. Include success, uncertainty/failure, and the relevant interruption path. Check hospital skips across fixed dates, low-rapport variants, skipped optional reading, and repeated ambient content where they apply.

A useful manual check asks: “If I do not send this message, does anyone later reply to it?” “If I never see this letter, can I still understand the next essential choice?” “If I leave early, what story do I receive instead?” These test actual behavior rather than the presence of headings in a plan.

For implemented narrative logic, leave the smallest runnable regression check that demonstrates the consequential behavior. A prose-only skill or scene needs editorial and path review, not a new test framework.

## Maintain the right document

- Current decisions, alternatives, and sortie seeds: story workshop.
- Accepted campaign developments and object continuity: story bible/current continuity sections, respecting their warnings about legacy material.
- Opening life and voice: that character's dossier and, if needed, their existing skill.
- Verified historical evidence and limits: historical research ledger.
- Interaction rules: mechanics design; actual runtime behavior must be checked in code when relevant.
- Reusable craft guidance: this skill. Keep it free of a second copy of the evolving plot.

A brainstorming answer may remain an answer. Do not record all suggested alternatives as events that happened. When the user asks for a canon change, update affected current references within scope and identify remaining conflicts rather than rewriting unrelated older drafts wholesale.

## Behavioral acceptance prompts

Use these for a manual regression pass when revising this skill. They are checks of decisions, not wording templates; do not add their proposed scenes to canon.

| Prompt | Behavior to inspect |
|---|---|
| “Plan the whole Flanders-to-Somme arc.” | Current aircrew roles, causal relationship development, player actions, unresolved postings labelled |
| “Write Tom's opening desk scene with his lucky sixpence.” | Identifies the timing conflict; offers a pre-sortie keepsake or a later scene without silently changing continuity |
| “Write a lively radio exchange with Will during the shoot.” | Preserves the intended exchange through available channels; no invented two-way radio |
| “Make Pike's first mission moving even if I abort.” | Pike contributes useful evidence; abort produces aftermath and a next beat |
| “Write a short letter from Vale to Margaret.” | Reads Vale's skill and dossier, uses domestic register, avoids forcing campaign exposition |
| “Give low-rapport players the cold version of the ending.” | Tone differs while essential story and payoff remain reachable |
| “Critique this single line from Rooke.” | Specific voice/context diagnosis without an unsolicited campaign outline |

Validation should report what was actually checked. A frontmatter validator cannot establish that dialogue is convincing or a branch works in the running game.
