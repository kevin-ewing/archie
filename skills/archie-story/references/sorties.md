# Sorties, choices, and agency

Use with the current workshop and relevant mechanics sections. Mission design should produce an experience the player can enact, not merely a dramatic synopsis followed by a flight.

## Turn a relationship beat into work

Start from what the crew is trying to accomplish. Introduce a difficulty that makes an established habit insufficient: obscured fall of shot, competing landmarks, a delayed acknowledgement, a hurried copy. Decide what evidence the player can actually perceive before asking them to judge.

A compact sortie brief can contain the following. Omit fields irrelevant to an early seed; include them when handing off a playable design.

| Field | What to specify |
|---|---|
| Placement | Chapter/date window, current observer, relevant prior events; distinguish fixed from conditional |
| Job | Operational objective and why someone needs the result |
| Human pressure | What each participant wants now; why reasonable aims may conflict |
| Entry | Orders or desk artifact, initial knowledge, any required setup |
| In flight | Observable evidence, observer contribution, ground response, player actions |
| Decision | Available options, known costs, what remains uncertain |
| Outcomes | What happens on each option, incomplete work, abort, and injury where applicable |
| Aftermath | Specific log, chart, letter, object, or changed routine that acknowledges the outcome |
| Continuation | Next beat, rejoining point, state carried forward, fallback for missed setup |
| Dependencies | Historical checks, world features, and features proposed rather than already built |

Do not fill a 10–15 minute sortie with uninterrupted exposition. Leave flying and noticing time. Introduce a new mechanic through a useful task; let its later use change relational meaning. The first week's one-mechanic-per-day design is a planning aid, not permission to repeat a tutorial after the player has learned it.

## Design choices the player can understand

A choice needs an intention the player can recognize and a consequence the game can remember. Operational choices and expressive choices can both matter. A reply may change tone without altering the mission; describe that honestly rather than promising a new plot branch.

Give plausible options for Tom, not an obvious virtuous answer beside a gratuitously cruel one. Distinguish “I need more evidence” from “I want to avoid responsibility.” Do not tell the player they knew something the interface never showed. Telegraph foreseeable costs such as another circuit consuming light; leave genuine uncertainty uncertain.

Separate choice quality from luck. An honest uncertain report need not always produce a successful shoot. A lucky estimate need not erase the risk taken. Later writing can acknowledge the decision and the result separately, without grading the player's morality.

Other characters may correct, refuse, ask, delay, or act within their roles. Do not make them agree with every player response. Rooke's agency still respects the distinction between receiving reports and battery command.

## Keep channels honest

Write the player's intended message separately from its signal encoding when that encoding is unsettled. Use panels for the ground reply, with author notes for any unverified code. A novel panel shape needs setup or a credible route to interpretation before it carries essential information.

Silence can create uncertainty, but it is not proof of death, anger, or abandonment. Supply clues and a later resolution appropriate to what occurred. The player must have a way to continue if a panel was obscured, a message interrupted, or an optional letter unread.

The player chooses reports; this does not settle who physically keys them. Do not silently install two-way radio or uninterrupted cockpit speech to make exposition convenient.

## Branch economically and remember honestly

Prefer shared major beats with meaningful local variations when they meet the dramatic need. Branch where subsequent experience actually changes. At reconvergence, preserve the fact of what happened through a changed line, task, artifact, or relationship response. Do not merge incompatible histories into a common letter.

For a consequential beat, write a small state table in prose or Markdown before inventing software:

| Trigger/prerequisite | What the player encounters | Result remembered | Next encounter/fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar, condition, or ambient opportunity | Actual content or event | Only facts needed later | How it continues if completed, skipped, or interrupted |

Separate historical/calendar beats, relationship conditions, and repeatable ambient material. Specify whether a beat occurs once, expires, or can be delivered later. Prevent ambient jokes from playing over casualty news, repeated first introductions, letters from people no longer available, and replies to messages never sent. Exact scheduling logic belongs to implementation only when requested.

Rapport must not lock the main story or ending. Let distant, strained, and close versions each make emotional sense. Optional reading can deepen meaning; essential comprehension needs another reliable route.

## Make failure part of the causal story

For the applicable paths, follow the next encounter rather than stopping at the immediate result:

- Good observation or completed work: what changed beyond a success label?
- Inconclusive or mistaken observation: what can still be reported, learned, or corrected?
- Voluntary withdrawal or early termination: how are partial results and the decision recognized?
- Wounding or hospital absence: what dates pass, what arrives by post, and what has changed on return?
- Missed conditional beat or unread artifact: how does the main story remain intelligible?

Calendar progress happens through play, including authored hospital time, never because the user left the game closed. A hospital skip must not strand essential setup on a date the player cannot visit. Do not punish ordinary caution by withholding the relationship's entire arc.

## Example: developing an existing seed

**Proposal, not a settled sortie:** In the workshop's missed-burst seed, smoke hides the fall of shot. The observer can report the limit of what was visible. The player can request another observation, send a qualified estimate if the communication design supports it, or leave with an incomplete report if conditions require it. Specify those costs before offering the choices.

At the desk, compare the message actually sent with the observer's chart. A repeated observation can cost light without becoming foolish; an estimate can be lucky without becoming reliable evidence. Each path leaves material for the next exchange. Do not automatically award intimacy for success, manufacture casualties for every error, or decide the sixpence letter's wording while fleshing out this seed.

## Test the experience

Walk through at least one ordinary path and the materially different interruption or failure paths. Ask what the player sees, understands, does, and encounters next. Use a paper walkthrough for a story proposal; use the running game only when implementing or testing an actual interaction. Pay attention to mobile reading, interrupted sessions, retrievable correspondence, and equivalent essential information when sound or subtle visual cues cannot be used. Flag missing presentation support instead of claiming it exists.
