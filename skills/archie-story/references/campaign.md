# Campaign and meaning

Use for the whole story, a chapter, a relationship arc, an ending, or a question about why the experience matters. Read the live workshop and bible first. The following are craft tools and proposals, not additions to canon.

## Find the dramatic question in the work

Separate the historical situation, the external job, and the human question. “Observe artillery in 1915” supplies activity and setting; it does not yet supply a dramatic argument. The cast workshop's question about certainty becomes dramatic when truthful doubt costs time and misplaced confidence can endanger other people.

For a proposed arc, identify:

- What a person wants and what habit currently helps them get it.
- Where that same habit becomes costly to someone else.
- What evidence or relationship could make another way of acting possible.
- A later decision that tests that possibility under changed conditions.
- What the player can do, and what other people or the war can still decide independently.

Do not reduce a person to a flaw that needs curing. Tom's care over finished work is useful; the problem can be what disappears when he tidies a doubtful report. Vale's precision can coexist with an omission that leaves Tom unable to judge. Pike can need confidence and still notice something the experienced men miss. Rooke can solve an actual delay while failing to show the aircraft why it is waiting.

These are candidate pressures supported by the dossiers, not guaranteed character transformations. Growth may be uneven, resisted, or expressed differently on different player paths.

## Build causality across scales

A campaign turn should change the circumstances of the next chapter. A chapter turn should grow from its sorties. A sortie should leave a trace that changes a subsequent action or interpretation. If beats can be shuffled without changing anything, examine their causal links.

Outline only as deeply as requested. A useful campaign table can hold:

| Phase/date | Relationship before | Pressure and player action | Others' decisions | Lasting consequence | Later payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proposed phase | What each expects of the other | What tests that expectation | What the player cannot dictate | Changed routine, knowledge, object, or circumstance | Where it returns in a new context |

Keep established events distinct from proposed ones. Label dependencies such as “requires Rooke to transfer south.” Historical events set conditions; the player need not be able to change history to affect another person's experience of it.

Design the chapter transition around both loss and continuity. Flanders-to-Somme changes landscape and observer. If Rooke remains the contact, a familiar signal can provide continuity; if he stays behind, correspondence can carry that bond. Compare the emotional effect before choosing. Do not import the old two-ground-operator arc by renaming its participants.

## Make mentorship reciprocal

Track what each person teaches, needs, misunderstands, and eventually does differently. Give Vale a decision with consequences, including the possibility of owning his own mistake. Give Pike evidence the player needs. Give Rooke actions that affect the shoot even though the player never sees his private scene.

A lesson passed on should become an action the player can choose in changed circumstances. Repeating the mentor's sentence is weaker than recognizing why his method helped and adapting it to someone with different strengths. Pike need not become Vale, and Tom need not always choose patience for the story to continue.

Separate professional trust, familiarity, and affection in the writing even if the game represents relationship state simply. A cold but reliable exchange can matter. A warm letter need not excuse a dangerous report. Do not require new numerical systems just to express those distinctions.

## Pace attachment and pressure

Across the intended 4–6 hours and roughly 20–25 flying days, vary the kind of work and emotional demand. Establish the ordinary version of a routine before changing it. Give the player time to learn a landmark, recognize a handwriting habit, or anticipate an acknowledgement before absence carries weight.

Use quiet beats for pleasure, curiosity, companionship, competence, or domestic life—not solely as ominous preparation. After an intense event, allow a response to arrive indirectly or later. Avoid stacking deaths and disasters merely to raise the stakes. Sometimes having to write a faithful report after a minor mistake is the harder scene.

Treat repeated scenery as remembered experience. A field or building matters because the player has used it, lost it, or returned to it. A generated location needs stable narrative relationships and a fallback if a particular feature is absent; do not write a landmark into every seed without checking how the world supplies it.

## Set up and repay emotional promises

For an important motif, know its first practical use, the relationship it acquires, and the later change. The player's accumulated memory should supply part of the meaning. Avoid explaining the motif every time it appears.

The sixpence has an established ownership chain; its significance should come from Rooke, the exchange, and Tom's care. It must not become a collectible whose value is completion percentage. Other ordinary things may matter without gaining a major payoff.

For an ending proposal, test:

- Which central relationship question receives an answer through action?
- What earlier experience makes the final gesture legible?
- What does the player still choose, and what separation or limitation can they not undo?
- What survives the loss: a practice, a correspondence, an obligation, a capacity to trust?
- Does it work after failure, a distant relationship, or missed optional letters?
- Does it respect the current crew and distinguish the campaign's ending from a possible postwar coda?

Do not settle the open ending while answering an unrelated scene request. When asked to choose an ending, offer a concrete recommendation with its required setup and costs. Bittersweet does not require a death, universal forgiveness, or a speech that explains the war.
