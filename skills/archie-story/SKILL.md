# Writing Archie

Make the player care about particular people through the work they do together, the decisions they share, and what remains afterward. Aim for emotional truth and playable consequences. Profundity is an effect of an experience the player has understood and participated in; it is not a requirement that characters speak profoundly.

This is the project-wide craft guide, not a second story bible. Read current source files rather than treating this skill's examples as new canon. All paths below are relative to this file.

## Start at the requested scale

Read the [workshop](../../docs/story/README.md) first, then the applicable sections of the [story bible](../../docs/STORY.md). Establish the date or chapter, requested deliverable, viewpoint, medium, and relevant prior choices. Infer ordinary details from the request; make a small, labelled assumption when needed. Ask only about a missing decision that materially changes the work. A question about one line should receive an answer about that line, not an unsolicited campaign redesign.

Use the appropriate reference, combining them when the task crosses scales:

| Task | Read | Deliver something usable |
|---|---|---|
| Theme, premise, campaign, chapter, relationship arc, ending | [Campaign and meaning](references/campaign.md) | A causal proposal with player involvement, emotional progression, and unresolved decisions |
| Mission, tutorial, encounter, branching choice, failure, narrative systems | [Sorties and agency](references/sorties.md) | A playable sequence with observations, decisions, consequences, and continuation paths |
| Scene, conversation, letter, log, panel beat, object, environmental detail | [Scenes and voices](references/scenes.md) plus each relevant character skill | The actual scene or text in its intended medium |
| Critique, revision, continuity question, diagnosing a flat moment | [Review and continuity](references/review.md) | Specific evidence, the underlying problem, and the smallest useful repair |
| Craft research or a problem needing another approach | [Source map](references/sources.md) | Apply a relevant technique to Archie; distinguish the source's teaching from our adaptation |

For brainstorming, offer meaningfully different directions and explain what each would make the player experience. Recommend when the evidence supports it. For drafting, write the requested material. For critique, diagnose before rewriting. Keep design notes and verification flags outside player-facing prose.

## Source authority and continuity

- Current explicit user decisions govern the task. For repository continuity, the workshop's carried-forward decisions and the bible's current-cast section supersede the older cast, single-seat premise, and ground-operator handoff—even where the old section says “Locked decisions.” Workshop alternatives and proposed character turning points remain proposals.
- The [cast workshop](../../docs/story/CHARACTERS.md) supplies relationship tensions and possible developments. Dossiers supply opening histories and voices. Preserve their stated draft status; do not silently promote a proposed biography or event to settled canon.
- Read [mechanics](../../docs/MECHANICS.md) for the relevant interaction, and [Rook Field](../../docs/AERODROME.md) for delivery at the desk. Distinguish design intent from implemented behavior. If asked to integrate writing into the game, inspect the actual caller and content structure; a design document alone does not prove a feature exists.
- Consult the [historical ledger](../../docs/HISTORICAL_RESEARCH.md) for period claims. Keep unverified ranks, procedures, equipment, vocabulary, and postings marked **[VERIFY]** in author notes. Research the exact date, place, and installation when precision matters. Plausible fiction is not historical evidence; a related aircraft or an object precedent does not establish the precise scene.
- A dossier stops immediately before that person's introduction and ends with one civilian writing sample. Put later events in campaign continuity. A newly drafted possibility does not become a past event merely because it appears in an answer.

## Project constraints that shape the writing

- Tom flies and positions the aircraft; Vale or Pike observes; Rooke works on the ground. Who physically keys the wireless remains open. Preserve the player's choice of what to send without settling the physical handoff by accident.
- Aircraft wireless is one-way. Ground replies reach the player through panels in flight and writing afterward. Do not put a spoken radio conversation across that gap. Any airborne crew exchange must have a plausible, established means of being perceived; exact procedure can remain an author question.
- Rook Field begins with the player alone at the barn desk. Orders, charts, letters, photographs, and logs can do substantial dramatic work without adding a staged conversation or new interface.
- Track who saw, inferred, sent, received, read, and retained each fact or object. Rooke owns the sixpence before first contact, posts it after Tom's first artillery sortie, and Tom posts it back after the war. Use [sixpence continuity](../../docs/STORY.md#the-sixpence); letter dates and wording remain open. It is not Tom's opening keepsake.
- Vale's departure, Rooke's posting across chapters, exact wireless duties, and the ending's cost are unresolved. The older separation candidates are options to reassess against the current crew, not instructions to enact. A postwar coin return does not require an Armistice gameplay ending.
- Preserve the story-game design: judgement rather than reflex tests; assisted Morse rather than manual keying; no shown takeoffs or landings; no permadeath; no offline calendar progress. Sunset is the normal return ritual, with early exits and hospital continuations available.
- Rapport changes how the relationship is expressed, not access to the main story. Difficulty and reading speed must not decide who receives the emotional ending. Campaign length and sortie count are planning targets, not reasons to pad a small request.

## Emotional standard

Use the cast's current working question—how much certainty do you owe people waiting on your word?—when it illuminates the request. Test competing answers through decisions and consequences. Do not force every domestic letter or joke to discuss reporting, doubt, or the war.

Earn attachment before threatening it. Let people be competent, mistaken, funny, bored, affectionate, and occupied with lives the player does not control. Give allies their own decisions. A rookie is not a device for praising the player; a mentor is not a dispenser of correct lessons; a ground contact is not a reward mechanism.

Build meaning through specific recurring actions whose context changes: making room on a chart, waiting for a signal, keeping an enclosure, returning a borrowed object. Allow practical things to remain practical. Show beauty alongside damage without making destruction decorative or presenting casualties as a measure of success.

Alternate pressure with ordinary work, relief, and space to absorb what happened. Restraint does not mean uniformly clipped prose or permanent emotional concealment. A direct admission can be earned. Avoid turning every ending into an aphorism, every object into a symbol, or every separation into a death.

## Finish at the same scale

Check continuity, character voice, player comprehension, and the promised consequence before delivery. For branches, follow the paths through their next encounter; for prose, read aloud for rhythm and audience. Use [review](references/review.md) for a deeper pass when needed.

Keep new proposals visibly separate from established facts. If editing repository material, update the appropriate source of truth only within the user's requested scope; preserve unrelated drafts and work in progress. State meaningful assumptions and remaining decisions briefly. Deliver the writing or answer, not the internal checklist.
