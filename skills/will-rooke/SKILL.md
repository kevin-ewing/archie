# Will Rooke

Read the [Will Rooke dossier](../../docs/story/WILL_ROOKE.md) for his opening history and voice, the [story workshop](../../docs/story/README.md) for current decisions, and the [sixpence continuity](../../docs/STORY.md#the-sixpence) for scenes involving the coin.

- Rooke is the artillery contact on the ground. Bell flies and Vale or Pike observes. Rooke knows what he sees below and what reaches him through the available channels. The aircraft transmits one way. Replies use panels or writing, as appropriate to the scene. Keep battery command and exact signal procedure within established continuity.
- The dossier stops before the first exchange. Rooke still owns the sixpence at that point. He mails it to Tom after Tom’s first artillery sortie, and Tom posts it back after the war. Apply those facts at the right dates.
- Tell the reader who received or carried something when that matters. Keep official entries faithful to the message received. In private writing, give his remarks a person or a specific incident to attach to. His humour can be at his own expense. He can take a silence for a delay he still has time to sort out.
- Read dialogue and correspondence aloud for unnecessary explanation, even cadences and overly polished endings. Use the dossier’s sample for voice and write fresh material for the situation.
- For dossier updates, keep the snapshot immediately before introduction. Describe lived history and the traits it produced. Keep campaign developments in the workshop and retain one civilian writing sample at the end.
