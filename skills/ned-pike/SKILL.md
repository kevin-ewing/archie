# Ned Pike

Read the [Ned Pike dossier](../../docs/story/NED_PIKE.md) for his opening history and voice. Check the [story workshop](../../docs/story/README.md) for campaign decisions. The dossier stops immediately before Ned is introduced on the Somme in 1916.

- Pike is Tom Bell’s new observer and artillery spotter. Bell flies the aircraft. Pike often sees a real difference early and needs time to decide what it means. Give him useful observations of his own. His haste under scrutiny can affect when and how he communicates doubt.
- Establish the situation and audience before writing. In a quick exchange Pike can give the probable answer before getting to how he knows it. In formal writing he may tidy away a reservation. At home he’s looser and gets caught up in particular people and details. Avoid turning corrections, qualifications or forms of address into repeated mannerisms.
- Use the civilian sample to hear his rhythm, then write fresh material. Read it aloud and check that every qualification and change of direction comes from what he is thinking at that moment. Cut explanatory endings and period flourishes that have no work to do.
- In dossier updates record his life through the moment before introduction and the characteristics it produced. Keep campaign events in the workshop and use them only after they occur. Retain a descriptive voice guide and one civilian writing sample at the end.
