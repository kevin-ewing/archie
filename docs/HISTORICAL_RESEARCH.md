# Historical research · 17 November 2026

Sources for Archie’s fictional crews, 1915–16 setting and equipment. The character dossiers record what has happened in the fiction. This page records the evidence for the setting and the limits of each comparison.

## Cast and voice

| Subject | Evidence and application |
|---|---|
| **Tom’s rank and route** | The June 1915 Gazette appoints both a Lieutenant and a Second Lieutenant from existing regiments as RFC Flying Officers. Second Lieutenant is defensible for Tom, and the appointment was distinct from substantive rank. His original unit, commission date, instruction and probation status remain open. The Central Flying School was one period route, not a service record for Tom. [Gazette, 18 June 1915](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/29197/page/5875/data.pdf) · [Raleigh, The War in the Air, ch. VIII](https://readingroo.ms/2/8/2/2/28226/28226-h/28226-h.htm#CHAPTER_VIII) · [National Archives research guide](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/officer-royal-flying-corps/) |
| **Vale’s 1915 observer experience** | William Leefe Robinson transferred from the Worcester Regiment to the RFC as an observer in March 1915. This supports an Army-to-observer route and an experienced observer serving with a separate pilot. No employer, rank, prior sortie or transfer date has been chosen for Vale. [RAF Museum, Leefe Robinson](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/for-valour-lieutenant-william-leefe-robinson-vc/) · [RAF Museum, contemporary aircrew accounts](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/aircrew-opinions-on-aerial-reconnaissance/) |
| **Pike’s summer 1916 instruction** | The official history describes variable early instruction and some observers going to Brooklands for wireless and to Hythe for aerial gunnery from 1916. The museum’s published research discusses the 1915 *Notes for Observers*. Keep Pike’s prior instruction and practice. Leave a named school, standardized qualification, rank and exact timeline open. [Raleigh, ch. VIII, pp. 443–444](https://readingroo.ms/2/8/2/2/28226/28226-h/28226-h.htm#CHAPTER_VIII) · [RAF Historical Society Journal 54](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/documents/Research/RAF-Historical-Society-Journals/Journal-54.pdf) |
| **Rooke’s civilian background and military bridge** | Sorting Clerk and Telegraphist was a real combined civilian grade. In 1913 Parliament discussed Royal Engineers “K” Telegraph Company men doing Post Office duties and moving into civilian posts. This documents overlap between the institutions. It does not establish Rooke’s fictional transfer, whether a Grantham clerk did counter and telegraph work under that grade, or who commanded panels at a given battery. Keep his rank and affiliation provisional. [Postal Museum, Post Office roles](https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/collections/post-office-roles/) · [Hansard, Royal Engineer Telegraphists, 1913](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1913/jul/11/royal-engineer-telegraphists) |
| **Military address** | The 1914 infantry manual records formal conduct when a soldier addresses an officer. The Gazette also distinguishes rank from appointment. These sources do not settle what two aircrew of unspecified ranks called each other in a noisy cockpit. Keep surnames and occasional *sir* as draft choices. Finalize forms of address once the rank relationships and scene are known. [War Office, Infantry Training, 1914](https://djvu.online/file/MKTkvp4val2DI) · [Gazette, 18 June 1915](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/29197/page/5875/data.pdf) |
| **Period English and regional voice** | Arnold Bennett’s 1908 *The Old Wives’ Tale* attests *a bit* and *I dare say*, including the latter in a domestic letter. It’s fiction set in the Potteries and a check on period register, not evidence for any of the four men’s local inflection. The dossiers’ accents are performance directions. Other distinctive idioms and claims about local usage still need a dated source before being treated as documented. [Bennett, The Old Wives’ Tale](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5247/pg5247-images.html) |

## Aircraft, guns and the desk

- **Clock code and replies.** Raleigh, pp. 341–342, gives target-centred rings Y/Z/A/B/C/D/E/F at 10/25/50/100/200/300/400/500 yards, with twelve at true north. He describes coded replies laid out in white strips and gives O.K. for a direct hit. Treat Archie’s Y/Z success tolerance as a game rule. Raleigh reproduces a September 1914 wireless shoot with short corrections, useful as a period example from before the later code. The precise 1915 panel alphabet, timings and calls remain open. [Raleigh, ch. VI](https://readingroo.ms/2/8/2/2/28226/28226-h/28226-h.htm#CHAPTER_VI)
- **Physical clock-code aids.** A celluloid overlay is supported by published research. A separate study reproduces Lewis describing discs with a narrower range of rings. That is evidence for the physical aid and for variation over time. The game card uses the full set in Raleigh. [British Commission for Military History, pp. 190–191](https://www.bcmh.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/WesternFront.pdf) · [Sanders, British artillery tactics, University of Adelaide](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/102d5f80-4e87-4358-bf33-8443ccc0ac67/content)
- **Crew and wireless.** A contemporary B.E.2c pilot’s account names his observer and describes the aircraft’s stability. Contemporary notes describe a 90 hp RAF engine. The AWM wireless transmitter is a material reference for airborne transmission. Together with the documented ground strips these support the channel design. How a specific 1915 or 1916 two-seat installation was arranged, who keyed, and the rigged aerial’s exact handling remain separate questions. [RAF Museum aircrew accounts](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/aircrew-opinions-on-aerial-reconnaissance/) · [Cornell’s 1917 rigging notebook](https://transcribe.awm.gov.au/transcription/item/awm201922242-12) · [AWM wireless spark transmitter](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C111197)
- **Busk and hands off.** Raleigh describes Busk intentionally releasing the controls in an R.E.1 dive and records his fatal November 1914 flight in an experimental B.E.2c. That source supports Busk’s stability work and the R.E.1 demonstration. The game’s hands-off mode is a control aid. The claim of a particular hands-off B.E.2c demonstration still needs its own flight record. [Raleigh, ch. III, pp. 165–167](https://readingroo.ms/2/8/2/2/28226/28226-h/28226-h.htm)
- **Sixpence.** The AWM catalogues a pendant made from an 1874 British sixpence in the collection of Great War serviceman A. M. Nicholson. It is an object precedent for a coin pendant associated with a serviceman. When it was converted or carried and whether it was considered lucky are separate questions. Will’s family history, signal cord and exchange with Tom are fiction. [AWM, Nicholson sixpence pendant](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1227013)
- **Desk and reconnaissance.** George V definitive stamps existed before the opening date. The Postal Museum identifies the 1911–12 red penny Downey Head issue and shows the 1912 Mackennal design. These are design references, with postage and markings for military correspondence to check separately. The RAF Museum has aircrew logbooks and discusses hand-held and fixed aerial cameras. Exact desk logbook cover, stamp on a specific military envelope, and camera model for a named sortie remain open. [Postal Museum stamp design](https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/collections/highlights/philatelic-collection/designing-a-royal-stamp/) · [Postal Museum issued Downey Head values](https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/blog/stamps-that-never-were/) · [RAF Museum logbooks](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/our-collection/collections/archive-collection/aircrew-logbooks/) · [RAF Museum aerial photography](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/documents/podcasts/transcripts/ww1_photo_recon.pdf)
- **Front maps and trench art.** British military maps used numbered grid squares with further lettered subdivisions. Use dated sheets from the intended sector before generalizing local trench spacing or drawing a national side’s typical defences. The General Staff’s §17 gives British firing bays of 18–30 feet and traverses 9–12 feet thick. It describes the need for substantial winter revetment in Flanders clay. The AWM holds a wartime trench-art ashtray and an 18-pounder cartridge case. Those give object references while the precise case-base ashtray on an RFC desk in May 1915 remains unverified. [National Archives map guide](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/military-maps-first-world-war) · [General Staff trench manual, §17](https://314th.org/numbered-documents/0582-notes-for-infantry-officers-on-trench-warfare.pdf) · [AWM Cairnes ashtray](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C115187) · [AWM 18-pounder case](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1021300)
- **Loading facts.** The RFC’s 1912 founding, its August 1914 move to France, “Archie” for anti-aircraft guns, the B.E. initials and the Fokker’s synchronized machine gun are supported. Robinson flew a B.E.2c over Cuffley on 2–3 September 1916 and shot down SL 11. Use *airship* for that event. SL 11 was a Schütte-Lanz. [RAF Museum 1912 chronology](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/research-enquiries/history-of-aviation-timeline/british-military-aviation/1912-2/) · [RAF Museum, RFC in August 1914](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/a-few-of-the-first-the-rfc-august-1914/) · [Raleigh, chs. III and VI](https://readingroo.ms/2/8/2/2/28226/28226-h/28226-h.htm) · [Museum of Flight, Eindecker](https://www.museumofflight.org/exhibits-and-events/aircraft/fokker-eiii-reproduction) · [RAF Museum, SL 11](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/destruction-of-a-baby-killer/)
