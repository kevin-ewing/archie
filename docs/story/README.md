## Decisions we’re carrying forward

- **1915, Flanders.** Tom Bell is the pilot. Arthur Vale is his experienced observer and artillery spotter.
- **1916, the Somme.** The squadron moves to a different sector. Tom remains the pilot. Ned Pike takes Vale’s seat as the rookie observer and artillery spotter.
- **On the ground.** There’s a separate artillery contact in the field. The aircrew communicates with him during shoots. The established story device is one-way wireless from the aircraft, with ground panels and written correspondence for replies.
- **Keepsakes and post.** Tom already saves small things that belonged to people he cares about and sends things in return. During service, receiving, keeping and sending objects gives those relationships a physical trace. The person and the circumstances of the exchange give each object its value to him.
- **The sixpence.** The artillery contact mails Tom his lucky coin after Tom’s first artillery sortie. Tom posts it back after the war. [Continuity](../STORY.md#the-sixpence)
- **Between flights.** Rook Field starts with the player alone at the barn desk. Orders, letters, charts, photographs and logbook entries can carry most of the character writing.

Tom flies and positions the aircraft. Vale or Pike watches the target and the fall of shot, then offers an observation. The ground contact receives the report and works with the battery. Who physically keys the wireless remains open.

The older draft put the mentor and rookie on the ground. This pass puts both in the aircraft and gives the artillery contact a separate profile. His exact posting and whether he serves the same battery in both chapters are open.

## From a character beat to a sortie

For each idea, write down what the crew is trying to accomplish, what makes the decision difficult, what the player actually does, and what changes afterward. A letter, a mark on the chart or a missing name on the orders can show the consequence. A failed observation should still give us a next beat.

| Seed | In the air | Back at the desk |
|---|---|---|
| **First circuit, Flanders** | Keep a field marker in sight through a turn. The observer’s chart has marks on both sides of the field so the player can reacquire it. | The route card gets one pencilled correction, based on where the player lost sight. |
| **The missed burst** | A smoke bank hides the fall of shot. Report uncertainty and request another round, or send an estimate and see how the battery responds. | Compare the reporting log with the observer’s chart. Each choice gives the crew a different conversation on paper. |
| **The first chart in the Somme** | Use new landmarks with the rookie. His quick first mark is plausible, and he has a second landmark that could settle it if given another circuit. | His clean chart includes his uncertainty in the margin. The pilot can acknowledge it or ask for a clearer reference next time. |

These are scene and mechanics seeds. Sortie order, success conditions and difficulty can come once the relationships are clearer.

## Screenwriting references

- Give each character a goal the audience can infer from their actions, then give them a choice that can change the situation. [Scriptnotes on wants](https://johnaugust.com/2016/scriptnotes-ep-279-what-do-they-want-transcript) · [Scriptnotes on agency](https://johnaugust.com/2024/scriptnotes-episode-665-what-can-you-even-do-transcript)
- Test a character’s starting belief in a series of situations and bring it back under greater pressure. Archie can let the player make that choice and read its consequences afterward. [Craig Mazin on the central dramatic argument](https://johnaugust.com/2019/scriptnotes-ep-403-how-to-write-a-movie-transcript)
